# Run Bun! — Dog artwork generation and editing protocol

Read [the shared card artwork rules](../../../GEN.md) first. This guide adds the Dog-specific direction. Follow current user instructions first, then the repository's AI RULES.md. This guide does not authorize application code changes.

## 1. Character and emotional purpose

Dogs are terrifying predators from the bunnies' perspective. They eat Buns. Their appearance must communicate that danger immediately: evil-looking, mean, vicious, and sometimes outright demonic.

Do not soften them into adorable pets, friendly companions, or harmless comic mascots. The adorable Buns and the frightening Dogs belong to the same illustrated world, but have deliberately different emotional roles.

## 2. Size and relationship to the world

Dogs must be HUGE relative to Buns. Show their size in believable proportion to bunny-sized structures, doors, furniture, vehicles, and other objects in the scene.

Dogs must look powerful and formidable, not diseased, starved, or emaciated. Give them substantial muscle and healthy body mass; avoid exposed ribs, skeletal hips, and excessively thin waists or limbs. Distinct silhouettes must still convey physical power.

Use recognizable scale cues so a Dog reads as an enormous threat within the bunny world, not merely a normal-sized dog filling the camera frame. When Buns appear, preserve the same size relationship between them and the Dog.

Keep perspective, ground contact, and environmental proportions coherent. No fixed numerical size ratio has been specified; do not invent one as an established rule.

## 3. Face, eyes, teeth, and menace

Eyes should look evil or demonic. Red eyes are a strong option, but other sinister colors and treatments are welcome. Vary their design creatively while preserving an unmistakably threatening expression.

Give Dogs vicious teeth and dangerous jaws. Snarls, bared fangs, predatory stares, and aggressive posture should make their intent readable. Some Dogs may be more overtly demonic than others; keep them recognizably Dogs.

Match the gaze, jaws, posture, and action to the scene. Menace should come from the creature itself as well as its presentation.

Dog scenery should be foreboding, dark, vile, and evil, reinforcing the threat from the bunnies' perspective. Make the surroundings feel hostile and dangerous. Darkness describes the mood and lighting; retain readable detail, full color, and sharp focus throughout, as required by the shared rules.

Buildings and settlements can be beautiful, colorful, and well cared for. They do not all need to be ruined or evil-looking: the Dog brings darkness, storms, and menace into the setting. Use that contrast while retaining visible color and detail.

Show believable evidence of the Dog's destructive passage: chewed wood, bite marks, splintered gates, broken fences, crushed objects, or other damage appropriate to its size and actions. Malgrim's broken chain is an approved detail, not an accessory to repeat on every Dog.

Vary how Buns react across scenes. Sometimes show them running; sometimes cowering, watching fearfully from windows, or peeking from behind curtains. Keep them adorable and make their fear, shelter, gaze, and scale readable. Do not crowd every reaction into every image.

Include active chase scenes among the Dog cards. The Dog must visibly pursue a Bun or group of Buns: align its gaze, posture, route, and movement with the fleeing targets so the pursuit is unmistakable. Keep action sharp rather than using motion blur.

Use the Run Bun! title artwork as a direct visual reference for running poses; the inspected title reference is ../../../../OLD/Manual/Title Fixed.png, relative to this Dog folder. Capture energetic airborne strides and leaps: feet should rarely touch the ground in running scenes. This applies to both pursuing Dogs and fleeing Buns. Show believable limb positions, clearance, shadows, and displaced water or debris so they read as running rather than floating. Use the title for motion, not as a requirement to copy its armor, palette, or composition.

## 4. Steampunk treatment and variety

Dogs are only slightly steampunk. Use restrained, purposeful details that fit their bodies and the Victorian steampunk world. Do not bury their identity or threatening anatomy under arbitrary machinery and decorative gears.

Vary silhouettes, fur, markings, eyes, teeth, expressions, and steampunk details so individual Dogs are distinct. Their shared qualities are enormous scale and vicious predatory menace, not identical accessories or faces.

Give each Dog a distinct setting and situation as well as an individual design. Do not repeatedly reuse the same street, buildings, weather composition, pose, damage, or frightened-Bun arrangement.

Vary the scenarios themselves, not just the scenery. Dogs can be resting, feeding, guarding a lair, or storing prey as well as pursuing Buns. Do not make every card a chase. Airborne running guidance applies to running scenes, not resting or feeding poses.

Approved examples, requested for the next two images:
- A Dog lying in its lair among Bun bones and skeletons, holding a bone between its folded front paws and gnawing the last meat from it.
- A Dog in a dark forest dropping a half-eaten Bun into a hole it dug, saving the remains for later. Make the freshly dug hole, displaced soil, and depositing action clear.

These examples establish additional scenes of predation; they are not a template to repeat on every card. Keep each Dog's action, posture, surroundings, and evidence of feeding coherent, with the same powerful build and threatening character.

The Bun guide's cute family proportions, fingernails, and four-digit hand/foot rules are Bun-specific. Do not automatically apply them to Dogs.

## 5. Files, dimensions, and review

- Artwork path: Images/Dog/Name.png.
- CSV Type: Dog.
- Follow the shared filename, backup, CSV, and verification rules.
- Frame Dog.png is 825 × 1125. The current Draw.lua Dog bounds at that size are approximately X 51, Y 192, width 716, height 485.
- Dog artwork aspect ratio: 716:485 (approximately 1.4763:1), landscape. Install at 1432 × 970 PNG, twice the opening dimensions. These are Dog dimensions, not Bun dimensions.
- Generate for this landscape opening. Scale uniformly and trim only small excess as needed; never stretch width and height independently. Keep essential features clear of the frame's rounded corners and decorative overlaps.
- Check scale against the bunny world, threatening expression, eyes and teeth, restrained steampunk details, coherent anatomy and perspective, and the shared full-color and sharp-focus requirements.
