# Run Bun! — Chase artwork generation and editing protocol

Read [the shared card artwork rules](../../../GEN.md) first. Use [the Bun guide](../Bun/GEN.md) for Bun appearance and anatomy and [the Dog guide](../Dog/GEN.md) for the attacking Dogs. This guide adds Chase-specific direction; it does not authorize application code changes.

## 1. Identity and emotional purpose

Chase cards depict a Dog attack on Bun civilization while the pursuit is still unfolding. Show the scenario and the ongoing danger, not a successful escape, a rescue, a betrayal, or another resolved outcome. The viewer should not know whether the Buns will get away.

An enormous demonic Dog has invaded the Buns' town or city. It actively pursues its inhabitants while smashing, breaking, biting, or crashing through the surroundings. Make the attack affect the place and its population, rather than staging a monster portrait or an isolated encounter with one or two Buns.

Include multiple Buns scrambling through the scene, with distinct readable reactions and escape paths. Keep foreground Buns prominent, adorable, expressive, and emotionally engaging; distribute additional fleeing Buns through the environment so the attack feels larger than the foreground pair. Avoid an indistinct decorative crowd.

No Bun helps or hurts another Bun in Chase artwork. Do not show pulling a companion to safety, holding an escape route for someone, carrying a friend, shoving, tripping, abandoning someone as a deliberate sacrifice, or similar Nice/Nasty interactions. Each Bun reacts to the attack and tries to escape. Multiple Buns fleeing the same danger is not itself a rescue scene.

The distinction is the card's visual subject: Chase is the attack and pursuit scenario. Nice and Nasty concern helpful and harmful interventions. Do not turn an escape outcome into a Chase concept merely because a Dog appears in the background. Do not invent gameplay effects from the scene.

When Buns face an attacking Dog, show unmistakable terror rather than mild worry: wide fearful eyes, strained brows, open alarmed mouths, pinned or swept ears, and urgent fleeing poses. Vary expressions naturally while keeping faces adorable and readable; the threat must feel immediate. Preserve their cute steampunk hats, goggles, clothing, and accessories throughout the action.

## 2. Variation across the set

Vary time of day, weather, city district, indoor/outdoor setting, camera angle, and the way the Dog breaches or destroys the environment. Do not make every scene a nighttime thunderstorm or repeat the same two Buns running along a street.

Possible settings include markets, rooftops, libraries, workshops, plazas, canalside districts, and other inhabited steampunk locations. These are scenario directions, not an approved production roster. A library attack could show the Dog crashing through shelving, books and broken wood flying, and several Buns fleeing through different aisles. Preserve coherent spatial relationships and readable escape routes.

Dogs can get onto rooftops and hunt at night; a Dog can penetrate the city boundary and pursue Buns inside the market. These capabilities remain valid worldbuilding, but the previously created Roof Runner and Market Scramble images are NOT approved Chase templates. The user reclassified them because they do not contain the full combination of elements now required.

## 3. Characters, movement, and composition

Use installed approved Bun and Dog images directly as visual references. Preserve the Buns' shared ancestry, youthful cute proportions, individual fur and outfits, compact bodies, substantial rabbit haunches, and broad rabbit feet. Do not replace their lower halves with skinny human legs. Hands have exactly three fingers and one thumb, with fingernails rather than claws; feet have exactly four digits.

Dogs remain enormous, powerful, recognizably canine demonic predators with exactly two eyes, vicious teeth, and restrained sinister colors. Follow the Dog guide's slightly steampunk treatment. No candy-colored coats or decorative neon markings. Preserve the identity of any particular installed Dog used as a reference.

Use ../../../../OLD/Manual/Title Fixed.png as a direct reference for energetic running. Show airborne strides, credible leaps, swept ears, trailing clothes, displaced debris and water, and believable shadows and contact. Distinguish fleeing, stumbling, and falling from static poses or floating bodies.

Make the route and cause-and-effect readable: where the Dog is coming from, which structures it damages, where the Buns are running, and what still threatens them. Destruction must follow physical contact or another clear cause. Match gazes and movement directions. Doors, wheels, rails, axles, ropes, grips and other mechanisms must work plausibly.

Vary how much of the Dog is visible without losing the town-attack scenario. A partial Dog view must still communicate scale, pursuit and destruction. Do not fall back on a lone Bun and a disembodied threat as the entire scene.

Keep full color and the established detailed illustrated finish. Beautiful steampunk settlements can contrast with the destruction brought by the Dog. Preserve readable faces and action in dark scenes. Everything must remain sharp, including distant scenery: no motion blur, bokeh, or blurred backgrounds. No baked-in text, card frame, or labels.

## 4. Dimensions and installation

- Artwork path: Images/Chase/Name.png.
- CSV Type: Chase.
- Current Draw.lua Chase bounds at the 825 × 1125 base card size: approximately X 46, Y 190, width 732, height 484.
- Artwork aspect ratio: 732:484 (183:121), landscape.
- Install at 1464 × 968 PNG, twice the opening dimensions.
- Scale proportionally and trim only small excess if necessary. Never stretch the artwork independently along each axis.
- Keep important faces, limbs, damage and pursuit details clear of frame overlaps and rounded corners.
- Follow the shared backup, revision-preservation, registration and verification requirements. Image backups belong in the artwork folder's Backups subfolder; retain older archives where they are.
- Preserve existing descriptions and flavor text unless explicitly authorized to change them. Do not populate them from concept notes automatically.

## 6. Chase-specific review

Before generating, identify the location, time, weather, Dog's attack path, physical destruction, multiple Buns and their individual flight paths. Check that the central idea is an ongoing attack, not an intervention or an escape outcome.

Before installing, verify that the pursuit reads at card size; several Buns are visibly reacting and fleeing; no Bun is helping or harming another; the Dog's scale, route and destruction are coherent; anatomy, grips and machinery make sense; the outcome remains uncertain; and all scenery stays sharp. Compare concepts across the set for scenario variety. Report file checks separately from the user's in-Studio visual review.


Style continuity is mandatory: use installed approved Bun and Dog images as direct generation inputs, not merely verbal inspiration. Match their facial anatomy, proportions, fur rendering, material detail, color richness, and polished dimensional finish. The rejected library Chase samples are not style references: do not reuse their flattened cartoon faces, character designs, or illustration treatment. A new scene must look like it belongs beside the existing cards.


Use established Bun designs directly. Preserve their reference body proportions, faces, clothing and clothing coverage when changing poses; do not impose longer torsos, shorter bodies, or another anatomy redesign. No exposed upper legs or skirts riding up. Crowds may include kid Buns but must not all look like children; no daycare-attack scenario. Match the existing adult Buns rather than inventing a new adult template.

For this ten-scene Chase series, mix existing Dogs with newly designed Dogs, and do not repeat a Dog. Vary the Bun cast and clothing between scenes; direct Bun references establish the art style and anatomy, not an instruction to clone their characters or outfits. New Buns must belong to the established family style.
