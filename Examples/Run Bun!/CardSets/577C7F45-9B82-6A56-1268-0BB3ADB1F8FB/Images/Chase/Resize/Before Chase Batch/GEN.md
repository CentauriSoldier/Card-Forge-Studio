# Run Bun! — Chase artwork generation and editing protocol

Read [the shared card artwork rules](../../../GEN.md) first. Use [the Bun guide](../Bun/GEN.md) for Bun appearance and anatomy and [the Dog guide](../Dog/GEN.md) for the pursuing Dogs. This guide adds Chase-specific direction; it does not authorize application code changes.

## 1. Identity and emotional purpose

Chase artwork centers on the Buns' escape and the decisive moment of danger. Dog cards establish the predator; Chase cards make the viewer wonder whether the Buns will get away. Keep the Buns prominent, adorable, expressive, and emotionally readable rather than small decorations around a monster portrait.

Both kindness and cruelty happen during a Chase. Some Buns help others escape; some deliberately sacrifice another Bun to save themselves. Include teamwork, selfishness, betrayal, and desperate individual ingenuity across the set. Do not make every Bun heroic or every Chase a betrayal.

For helpful scenes, clearly show who is helping whom and how. For betrayal, show the betrayer's deliberate physical action, the victim's reaction, the approaching threat, and the escape advantage gained. A shove must look like a shove, not a rescue; a released grip must not look like an accidental slip. Keep the victim sympathetic and the selfish Bun within the same adorable family design, without turning it into a different monster species.

Capture the action while the outcome is still in doubt. These are pursuits, narrow escapes, rescues, and sacrifices during flight, rather than repeated feeding scenes or static Dog portraits. The threat can be frightening and the consequences cruel without relying on gore to explain every scene.

The helpful/selfish distinctions below describe artwork, not card rules or separate CSV types. All these cards remain Chase cards. Do not invent gameplay effects or fill Description and Flavor from the concepts.

## 2. Characters, movement, and composition

Use installed approved Bun and Dog images directly as visual references. Preserve the Buns' shared ancestry, cute proportions, and individual fur-and-outfit combinations. No elderly-looking Buns. Hands have exactly three fingers and one thumb, with fingernails rather than claws; feet have exactly four digits. Expressions can show terror, determination, shock, or selfish calculation without losing their appeal.

Dogs remain enormous, powerful, recognizably canine, demonic predators with exactly two eyes, vicious teeth, and restrained sinister colors. Follow the Dog guide's slightly steampunk treatment. Do not give them candy-colored coats or decorative neon markings. Preserve the established identity when depicting a particular installed Dog.

Use ../../../../OLD/Manual/Title Fixed.png as a direct reference for energetic running. Running feet should rarely touch the ground: show airborne strides and leaps with credible anatomy, trajectories, shadows, and clearance. Supported actions such as bracing a gate or hauling a rope still need believable contact.

Convey speed with diagonal compositions, stretched strides, swept ears, trailing coats, flying debris, splashing water, and damaged obstacles. Everything MUST remain in focus, including distant scenery. No motion blur, bokeh, or blurred backgrounds.

Make the route and cause-and-effect readable: where the Buns came from, where they are trying to go, what blocks them, and where the Dog is approaching. Match gazes and movement directions. Tools, vehicles, ropes, hinges, and mechanisms must work plausibly; hands must actually grip what they use.

Vary viewpoints, locations, escape methods, numbers of Buns, and how much of the Dog is visible. Some images may show only pursuing jaws, a giant paw, or a clearly canine looming shadow. Partial views must still communicate a real pursuit; do not make every scene a full-body Dog chasing two Buns down a street.

Keep full color and the established detailed illustrated finish. Attractive steampunk settlements can contrast with the darkness and destruction brought by the Dog. Preserve readable faces and action in dark scenes. No baked-in text, card frame, or labels.

## 3. Dimensions and installation

- Artwork path: Images/Chase/Name.png.
- CSV Type: Chase.
- The current Draw.lua Chase bounds at the 825 × 1125 base card size are approximately X 46, Y 190, width 732, height 484.
- Artwork aspect ratio: 732:484 (183:121), landscape.
- Install at 1464 × 968 PNG, twice the opening dimensions. Do not reuse the Bun or Dog dimensions.
- Scale proportionally and trim only small excess if necessary. Never stretch the artwork independently along each axis.
- Keep faces, interacting hands, the victim, and the crucial obstacle clear of frame overlaps and rounded corners.
- Follow shared backup, revision preservation, immediate CSV registration, and verification requirements for each installed image.

## 5. Chase-specific review

Before installing, check that the escape action reads at card size; the Buns remain the emotional focus; the helpful or selfish interaction is unambiguous where applicable; Dog scale and pursuit direction are consistent; anatomy, grips, and machinery make sense; and all scenery remains sharp. Compare each new scene with the roster to prevent repeated compositions. Report installation and file checks separately from any in-Studio visual check.
