# 🐇 Run **Bun**!

**3–8 players · 1 Dog · 2 Bunnies per Bunny player · 3 days**

A fast, loud steampunk **Chase**. Help a rival get away when the **Dog** is ahead.
Send the **Chase** their way when they’re pulling ahead of you.

## 🥕 The one-minute teach

One player is the **Dog**. Everyone else starts with two **Bunnies**, called a
**Brace**. The **Dog** draws cards and plays **Chase** to pursue a **Bunny**. Its player
can react with **Panic**, then both sides roll a d20. If the **Dog** wins, it
catches the **Bunny** and chooses to eat or injure it. If the **Bunny** wins,
it escapes for the day.

Every **Bunny** you get to safety keeps you in the action from the **Burrow**.
Injured **Bunnies** sit out until **Dawn**. Once every **Bunny** has escaped, been
eaten, or been injured, **Dusk** falls and everyone scores. At **Dawn**, escaped
and injured **Bunnies** return to their **Braces**, and fresh **Bunnies** take the
places of any the **Dog** ate. Get the same **Bunny** out again and its escape is
worth even more.

After Day 3, add up the scores. The highest total wins. Whether you played
the table to keep your **Brace** alive or timed your **Chases** to catch the
**Bunnies** that mattered most, three days of sharp decisions earned you the
win.

## Glossary

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Dog** | The character that pursues the **Bunnies**, or the player controlling it. |
| **Bun** / **Bunny** | Interchangeable terms for a character controlled by a **Bunny** player. |
| **Brace** | The pair of **Bunnies** belonging to one player. |
| **Burrow** | A section on a **Bunny** card. Each escaped **Bunny** gives its player up to one play using that section per **Dog** turn. |
| **Chase** | A **Dog** card that starts a pursuit. The pursuit itself is called a **Chase**. |
| **Lure** | A **Dog** card that baits a **Bunny** or sets up a **Chase**. |
| **Nice** / **Nasty** | **Nice** cards help another player's **Bunny**. **Nasty** cards make trouble for another player's **Bunny**. |
| **Panic** | A **Reaction** card you can play when one of your **Bunnies** is chased. |
| **Reaction** | A card played in response to an event. For example, **Panic** responds to a **Chase** targeting one of your **Bunnies**. |
| **Chase Modifier** | The value printed on a **Bun** card that adjusts its roll in a **Chase**. |
| **Redirect** | An effect that sends a **Chase** toward a different **Bunny**. |
| **Natural Roll** | The number showing on the die before modifiers. |
| **Dusk** / **Dawn** | At **Dusk**, score the day. At **Dawn**, reset the **Braces** for the next day. |
| **Last Chase** | The roll-off that decides the winner when the **Dog** ties for first. |

## What You'll Need

- **A d20.** Share one die for opposed rolls and injury penalties.
- **A score sheet and pencil** to track daily scores and each **Bunny**'s
  escapes.

## Setup

1. **Choose the Dog player.** The lowest-scoring player from the previous
   game plays the **Dog**. If this is the first game, everyone rolls a d20. If
   players tied for the lowest score, only those players roll. The lowest
   roll gets the **Dog**. Reroll among players tied for the lowest roll until
   one player gets it. Everyone else plays as a **Bunny** player.
2. **Establish turn order.** Start with the **Dog** player and go clockwise
   around the table. Use this order for the starting hands and **Bun** draws.
3. **Shuffle and lay out the decks.** Shuffle the **Dog** and **Bun** character
   decks separately and place them face down within reach. Shuffle **Chase**
   and **Lure** together to make the **Dog**'s draw pile. Shuffle **Nice**, **Nasty**, and
   **Panic** together to make the **Bunny** players' shared draw pile. Place both
   draw piles face down and leave space for a separate discard pile beside
   each.

## Start Playing

1. **Draw characters.** The **Dog** player draws one card from the **Dog** character
   deck and places it face up in front of them. Then, in clockwise order
   from the **Dog**, each **Bunny** player draws two **Bun** cards and places them face
   up in front of them. These two **Bunnies** form that player's **Brace**. Both
   start in play with no previous escapes.
2. **Draw starting hands.** In turn order, each player draws their starting
      hand from their side's draw pile. The **Dog** draws 5 cards or one per **Bunny**
      player, whichever is higher. Each **Bunny** player draws 5 cards.
3. **Start Day 1.** Begin with the first **Dog** turn. Keep a record of each
   day's scores and each **Bunny**'s escapes for later days.

## Take a **Dog** Turn 🐕

Draw cards, make plays and resolve any **Chases**, then discard down to the
current hand limits. Take another **Dog** turn if any **Bunnies** are still in
play. Once all **Bunnies** are out of play for the day, score at **Dusk**.

### Draw cards and check play allowances

The **Dog** draws **one card per Bunny player** each turn. At a five-player
table, the **Dog** draws four. The **Dog**’s maximum hand size is **5 or the number
of Bunny players, whichever is higher**. Its deck mixes **Chase** and
**Lure** cards, and both count toward the same **Dog** play limit. The size of
that play limit is still open.

A **Bunny** player’s maximum hand size is **3 + 1 for each Bunny in play or
escaped**. Each **Bunny**’s status also sets how many cards its player draws
and may play. A play requires an eligible card in hand.

| **Bunny** status | Draws per **Dog** turn | Maximum plays per **Dog** turn |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| In play | 1 | **Up to 1 regular play** |
| Escaped | 1 | **Up to 1 Burrow play** |
| Injured | 0 | **0 plays** |
| Eaten | 0 | **0 plays** |

With two **Bunnies** in play, you may make **up to two regular plays**. With one
in play and one escaped, you may make **up to one regular play and up to one
Burrow play**. Once both escape, you may make **up to two Burrow plays**, using
only the **Burrow** section of your cards. If the **Dog** **Chases** one of your **Bunnies**,
you may play **up to one Panic card** for that **Chase**, separately from these plays.

**Empty deck.** When a deck runs out, shuffle its discard pile to make a new draw pile. Continue any remaining draws.

### 🎲 Resolve a **Chase**

1. **Choose the target.** The **Dog** plays a **Chase** card and picks an eligible
   **Bunny** in play. A **Bunny** can be chased only once per **Dog** turn. How **Redirects**
   count toward that limit is still open.
2. **React.** The targeted **Bunny**’s player can play one **Panic** card. Apply
   any legal modifiers before rolling. The **Chase** goes ahead even if the
   **Bunny** player declines to react.
3. **Roll.** The **Dog** and **Bunny** each roll a d20. Check **Natural Rolls** first.
   If neither side rolled a **Natural 1** or **Natural 20**, compare the totals after the
   **Bunny's** printed **Chase** modifier and any card modifiers.

| **Natural Rolls** | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Both roll **1**, or both roll **20** | Both reroll. |
| **Dog** rolls **20**, or **Bunny** rolls **1** | The **Dog** catches the **Bunny**. |
| **Bunny** rolls **20**, or **Dog** rolls **1** | The **Bunny** escapes. |
| Neither rolls a 1 or 20 | Higher **Dog** total means a catch. Higher **Bunny** total means an escape. |

In a regular **Chase**, a **Natural 20** earns **+1 point** and a **Natural 1**
costs **1 point** for the side that rolled it. Apply both adjustments if
both sides qualify. The **Bunny** adjustment goes to the targeted **Bunny**'s
player. Count only the roll that resolves the **Chase**. Record these points
for **Dusk** in addition to the usual outcome scores.

**Example:** The **Dog** rolls **14**. The **Bunny** rolls **12** with a printed
**+3 Chase Modifier**. The **Bunny**’s total is **15**, so it escapes immediately.

**Bunnies** stay in play until chased. Winning a **Chase** is enough to escape for
the day. There are no Escape cards.

#### 🐕 Eat or Injure

When the **Dog** catches a **Bunny**, choose one.

- **Eat it.** Remove that **Bunny** from the game permanently. The **Dog** earns **+1**,
  the **Bunny** player loses **1 point**, and the usual **Brace** bonuses apply.
- **Injure it.** The **Bunny** is **injured** and sits out for the rest of the
  day. The **Dog** rolls a d20. A roll of **1–10** gives an injury multiplier
  of **1**. A roll of **11–20** gives a multiplier of **2**. Multiply
  **(previous escapes + 1)** by the injury multiplier
  and subtract that many points from the **Bunny**’s player. Count only escapes
  by that same **Bunny** on earlier days. The **Dog** scores no points for releasing it.

| Earlier escapes | **Bunny** player | **Dog** |
| ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 0 | **−1 or −2** | 0 |
| 1 | **−2 or −4** | 0 |
| 2 | **−3 or −6** | 0 |

An injured **Bunny** cannot be chased again that day and gives its owner no **Burrow** play.

### Finish the **Dog** turn

**End the turn.** After all plays and **Chases** for the full turn are finished,
each player discards down to their current maximum hand size. Use each
**Bunny**’s status at the end of the turn to calculate its player’s limit.

## 🌇 Score at **Dusk**

**Dusk** falls when every **Bunny** has **escaped, been eaten, or been injured**.
Score each **Brace** separately, include any **Natural Roll** adjustments from
its **Chases**, and record the result for that day.

| Outcome | **Bunny** player | **Dog** |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| **Bunny** escaped | **+2**, plus any repeat-escape bonus | 0 |
| **Bunny** injured | **−(previous escapes + 1) × injury multiplier** | 0 |
| **Bunny** eaten | **−1** | **+1** |
| Both **Bunnies** in the same **Brace** escaped that day | **+1 extra** | 0 |
| Both **Bunnies** in the same **Brace** were eaten that day | **−1 extra** | **+1 extra** |


A full **Brace** eaten scores **−3** for its player and **+3** for the **Dog**. The
**Dog** earns the same catch points whether a **Bunny** is fresh or has escaped
before.

### 🐾 Repeat escapes

Each time a **Bunny** escapes, add **+1 for each earlier day that same Bunny
escaped**.

| Escape by the same **Bunny** | Points |
| --- | ---: |
| First | **2** |
| Second | **3** |
| Third | **4** |

The daily **+1 Brace bonus** still applies. If the same two **Bunnies** escape
on all three days, they score **5 + 7 + 9 = 21 points**. A **Bunny** that escaped
on Day 1 and was injured on Day 2 gets **3 points** if it escapes on Day 3.
It has only one earlier escape.

### 🧮 Daily **Brace** score matrix

Read each row in either order. The scores include the daily **Brace** bonus or
penalty. Add any **Natural Roll** adjustments separately. For each injured **Bunny**,
use its actual penalty from **Eat or Injure**.

| One **Bunny** | Other **Bunny** | **Bunny** player | **Dog** |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: |
| Escaped | Escaped | **+5 to +9** | 0 |
| Escaped | Injured | **+2 to +4**, minus the injure penalty | 0 |
| Escaped | Eaten | **+1 to +3** | **+1** |
| Injured | Injured | **Subtract both injure penalties** | 0 |
| Injured | Eaten | **−1**, minus the injure penalty | **+1** |
| Eaten | Eaten | **−3** | **+3** |

For example, if a **Bunny** with one previous escape gets away and its partner
is injured with a 2-point penalty, the **Bunny** player scores **3 − 2 = 1** and
the **Dog** scores **0**.

## 🌅 Reset at **Dawn**

- **Escaped:** the same **Bunny** returns to play and keeps its escape history.
- **Injured:** the same **Bunny** returns to play and keeps its escape history.
- **Eaten:** that **Bunny** is gone for the rest of the game. Fill its slot with a new **Bunny** with no escape history.

For example, if one **Bunny** escaped and its partner was eaten on Day 1, you
start Day 2 with the **Bunny** that escaped and one new **Bunny**. Your Day 1 score
stays recorded.

## 🏁 Who wins

Add each player’s scores from all three days. **The highest score wins.**
**Bunny** players tied for first share the win. If the **Dog** ties for first with
one or more **Bunny** players, settle it with **Last Chase**.

The **Dog** can score from any **Brace**. That gives **Bunny** players a reason to
cooperate when the **Dog** is ahead. A veteran **Bunny** is also a tempting target
for rivals. On Day 3, a **Bunny** with two earlier escapes can score **+4** by
escaping or **−1** if eaten, even before the **Brace** bonus. A **Redirect** can
sabotage its owner or give them the escape they needed.

### 🎲 **Last Chase**

Each tied **Bunny** player chooses one **Bunny** from their **Brace** for a final opposed
d20 roll against the **Dog**. Use that **Bunny**’s printed **Chase** modifier and the usual
**Natural 1**, **Natural 20**, and reroll rules. No cards are played. **Natural Rolls**
settle the **Chase** outcome, and the final scores stay the same.

- **The Dog catches every tied Bunny:** the **Dog** wins.
- **One tied Bunny escapes:** its player wins.
- **Several tied Bunnies escape:** their players share the win.
